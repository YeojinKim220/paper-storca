## stORCA detects diverse spatial changes and ignores global shifts

Because existing differential spatial methods rely on distinct statistical assumptions, each method can miss specific geometries of expression change or mistake uniform shifts for spatial remodeling. Registration-based correlation scorers (CODA and STcompare correlation) are scale-invariant and therefore blind to pure amplitude changes. Domain-level tests (DESpace2) pool expression within domains and therefore miss redistributions that preserve domain totals. Learned-coordinate spline regressions (SpaceExpress) depend on shared latent axes that can smooth narrow or intra-domain shifts. Uncentered similarity or discriminative classifiers (STcompare percent similarity and River) respond to any difference between conditions, including uniform fold changes. To test these predictions directly, we evaluated stORCA and all six comparator outputs on two benchmarks with known ground truth: controlled perturbations in synthetic tissues (Fig. 2a to c) and anatomically aligned perturbations implanted into human dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) Visium sections across donors (Fig. 2d, e).

In the synthetic benchmark, we designed six spatial change scenarios spanning domain, cell-type, sub-domain, boundary, amplitude, and radial geometries (Fig. 2a). Five scenarios used a 60 × 60 lattice (3,600 cells) partitioned into four vertical domains (A to D, 15 columns each). The sixth (Ring domain) used a circular disk (3,640 cells within radius 34 on a 68 × 68 lattice) divided into four concentric rings. Each domain contained an equal 50:50 mixture of two of four cell types (CT0 to CT3), sampled independently for each section. Across three sections per condition and five effect strengths (s = 0 to 2.0), each tissue carried 120 negative binomial genes: 80 unchanged domain markers, 20 unchanged cell-type markers, 10 global-shift decoys shifted by +s on the log mean in every condition-2 cell, and 10 focal genes. Each scenario applied one focal change: Domain DE, CT DE in A, Within shift inside domain A, Border band at columns 14 and 15 on the A/B interface, Amp. change scaling the baseline pattern, or Ring domain. Every gene carried a baseline spatial pattern with matched amplitude bins (1.0 to 2.4), preventing detection by baseline spatial structure alone. Because five comparator scores test single section pairs, we ran stORCA in the same single-pair mode across three matched pairs, ranking all 120 genes by clipped ROS without gene filtering. DESpace2 received all six sections and the true domain labels, so its result is an upper bound for a domain test.

At intermediate effect strength (s = 1.0), stORCA was the only method that ranked focal genes well above unchanged genes across all six change types (Fig. 2b, left and Supplementary Fig. 2). stORCA achieved AUROC 0.86 to 1.00 (mean 0.96) and mean AUPRC 0.81 against a 0.09 random baseline, scoring 1.00 on Domain DE, Amp. change, and Ring domain, 0.98 on CT DE in A, 0.93 on Within shift, and 0.86 on Border band. Every comparator fell to 0.62 or below on at least one change type. DESpace2 reached 1.00 on the four changes altering a domain mean but dropped to 0.61 on Within shift and 0.46 on Border band, where domain-wide averages stay nearly invariant. SpaceExpress similarly scored 0.91 to 1.00 on domain-level changes while dropping to 0.43 on Within shift and 0.59 on Border band. CODA (0.22) and STcompare correlation (0.28) ranked Amp. change below unchanged genes because scaling raises signal-to-noise ratio without changing normalized correlation, while hovering near chance on the other five types (0.44 to 0.56 for CODA; 0.50 to 0.62 for STcompare correlation). STcompare percent similarity recovered five types (0.71 to 0.95) but fell to 0.54 on Border band, and River scored 0.18 to 0.52 (mean 0.30).

stORCA also separated true spatial changes from uniform global-shift decoys across scenarios and effect strengths (Fig. 2b, right and Fig. 2c). At s = 1.0, stORCA achieved the highest focal-versus-decoy separation (mean AUROC 0.94, range 0.74 to 1.00), placing 0.5 of 10 decoys and 8.5 of 10 focal genes on average in the top 15 of 120 genes. DESpace2 (0.82, 0.7 decoys), SpaceExpress (0.76, 1.4 decoys), STcompare correlation (0.64, 0.1 decoys), and CODA (0.62, 0.2 decoys) showed moderate-to-high separation. By contrast, River and STcompare percent similarity both scored 0.00 against decoys and placed all 10.0 of 10 decoys inside their top 15 in every scenario. Centering each expression field on kept cells removes any additive constant on the scoring scale, preventing global shifts from inflating ROS. Across the strength sweep (Fig. 2c), all methods sat near chance at s = 0 (stORCA AUROC 0.38 to 0.56, mean 0.48; comparators 0.33 to 0.61), so no method favored the designated genes before a change existed. At s = 0.5, stORCA reached 0.85 to 0.98 across five scenarios, while Border band rose from 0.50 (s = 0.5) to 0.86 (s = 1.0) and 0.91 (s = 1.5 and 2.0). By s = 2.0, stORCA ranged from 0.89 (Within shift) to 1.00 (mean 0.97). Over the same sweep, DESpace2 recovered Border band (1.00) only at high strength while remaining at 0.68 on Within shift, and CODA and STcompare correlation fell monotonically on Amp. change to 0.15 and 0.25.

To test whether these advantages hold against real donor and section variation, we implanted known perturbations into six human DLPFC Visium sections from three donors (3,563 to 4,783 spots per section, 3,000 shared highly variable genes; Fig. 2d, e). Each donor contributed one control section (151507, 151669, and 151673) and one treated section (151509, 151671, and 151675). Across three effect levels (L1 to L3), we implanted 50 positive genes (10 each of Domain DE, Within shift, Amplitude, Relocation, and Pure shift) alongside 20 global-shift decoys scaled identically in every layer. Unlike Within shift (which raises counts in one half of layer 3), Pure shift redistributes counts between halves of layer 3 while keeping the expected layer total constant. Strongly layered genes received Amplitude and Relocation, which need a laminar pattern to act on, and weakly layered genes received the three layer-3 changes, so that an existing layer-3 pattern did not hide them. To maintain anatomical correspondence across donors, we oriented the layer 3 split along the cortical tangent in every treated section and applied one cyclic permutation across the five shared layers (Layer3 through white matter). All methods were evaluated on the identical label-free universe tested by stORCA (664, 642, and 588 genes at L1, L2, and L3), retaining all 50 positives at L1 and L2, 49 at L3 after one attenuated amplitude gene (*MAP4K4*) fell below the spatial gate, and all 20 decoys. stORCA ran its replicate design (9 between-condition vs 6 within-condition pairs), pairwise comparators averaged scores over the 9 between-condition pairs, and DESpace2 used all six sections with the manual layer annotations defining four of the five perturbations.

At level 3 in human DLPFC, stORCA achieved the highest overall AUROC (0.92) and was the only method at or above 0.80 on all five change types (Fig. 2d, left). stORCA scored 1.00 on Domain DE, 0.99 on Within shift, 0.80 on Amplitude, 0.90 on Relocation, and 0.92 on Pure shift (AUPRC 0.44 vs a 0.02 baseline; Supplementary Fig. 2). Every comparator missed at least one perturbation geometry. DESpace2 reached 0.96 to 1.00 on the four changes altering a layer mean but dropped to 0.40 on Pure shift (AUPRC 0.02) because layer totals remained invariant. CODA and STcompare correlation missed Within shift (0.37 and 0.33), Amplitude (0.53 and 0.49), and Pure shift (0.38 and 0.28), while STcompare percent similarity stayed below chance on every type (0.28 to 0.41). SpaceExpress (0.72 to 0.98, overall AUROC 0.87) and River (0.72 to 0.96, overall AUROC 0.81) detected all five types moderately well, including Pure shift (0.72 and 0.78), yet produced much lower overall AUPRC (0.38 and 0.21 vs 0.70 for stORCA; Supplementary Fig. 2). Supplied with the ground-truth manual layer labels underlying four implant types, DESpace2 achieved slightly higher overall AUPRC (0.74, 34 positives in the top 50) than label-free stORCA (0.70, 29 positives in the top 50).

In human cortex, stORCA also kept global-shift decoys out of its top-ranked genes, and all of its calls were implanted genes (Fig. 2d, right). On focal positives versus global-shift decoys at level 3, stORCA reached AUROC 0.90 with 0 decoys in its top 50 ranked genes, ahead of DESpace2 (0.86, 0 decoys), STcompare correlation (0.72, 0 decoys), CODA (0.63, 3 decoys), and SpaceExpress (0.52, 7 decoys). By contrast, STcompare percent similarity (0.00, 6 decoys) and River (0.00, 20 of 20 decoys in the top 50 at every level L1 to L3) ranked decoys ahead of spatial perturbations, showing that River's detection AUROC reflects any group difference rather than spatial specificity. At the call threshold (one-sided empirical Bayes limma, Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted *P* < 0.05), stORCA called 8 genes at level 3, all 8 implanted positives: 6 Domain DE (*KDELR2*, *PNPLA2*, *SHOC2*, *WASF3*, *RPS19*, *RPS9*), 1 Within shift (*PSME1*), and 1 Relocation (*PCP4*). It made 0 calls among all 519 unchanged genes and 20 decoys, and 0 calls across all 9 non-true section-label reassignments at L1, L2, and L3.

At the two weaker implant levels (L1 and L2), rankings without a replicate baseline and a domain test given the true layers ranked implanted genes higher overall than stORCA's three-donor replicate test (Fig. 2e). At L1, overall AUROC was 0.47 for stORCA compared with 0.81 for River, 0.73 for DESpace2, 0.63 for SpaceExpress, 0.46 for CODA, 0.40 for STcompare correlation, and 0.23 for STcompare percent similarity. At L2, stORCA rose to 0.98 on Domain DE and 0.86 on Within shift (overall AUROC 0.72, with 0.67 on Amplitude, 0.57 on Pure shift, and 0.51 on Relocation), behind River (0.84), DESpace2 (0.82), and SpaceExpress (0.81); stORCA made 0 discrete calls at L1 and L2. This tradeoff stems directly from the replicate baseline: with three donors per group, stORCA calls a change only when between-condition divergence exceeds natural within-condition donor variation, whereas scores without a replicate baseline rank weak perturbations together with donor differences and global shifts.
