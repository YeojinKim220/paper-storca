## S1. Matching tissue and measuring spatial concordance

### S1.1. From cell states to a partial transport plan

For each ordered source–target comparison we intersected the feature panels, concatenated the cells in source-first order and fitted SysVI to the normalized, log-transformed input defined in main Methods, Section 1. The section identifier was the system covariate. In between-condition pairs that identifier also distinguished the two conditions. We used seed 42, a 15-dimensional latent state and the learned five-component VampPrior. We took posterior means after 400 epochs with batches of 128 for the DLPFC, HPOA, V1, ARTISTA, cortical injury and synthetic benchmark fits. For the larger colon comparisons we used 100 epochs and batches of 512. Section S1.6 specifies the neural objective and the tensors exchanged between encoder and decoder.

For cell $i$, the niche vector was the mean latent vector of the 20 nearest cells in its own spatial coordinates, including itself. We normalized the cell and niche vectors separately to unit length. For each branch we used $d=(1-\cos)/2$ and combined the distances as $C_{ij}=1-(1-d^{\rm cell}_{ij})(1-d^{\rm niche}_{ij})$. To estimate availability of counterparts, we counted source and target cells among the 50 nearest neighbors in the pooled **latent** space. For source and target counts $c_s$ and $c_t$, we computed $\ell=\log[(c_t+0.5)/n_t]-\log[(c_s+0.5)/n_s]$ and averaged this value over the focal cell and its 50 neighbors to obtain $\bar\ell$. We estimated one tolerance $\delta$ per replicate study as the 95th percentile of $|\bar\ell|$ pooled over cells of all within-condition pairs. For the synthetic single-pair design, $\delta=0$.

The source capacity was $a_i=\min(1,e^{\bar\ell_i+\delta})/n_s$ and the target capacity was $b_j=\min(1,e^{-\bar\ell_j+\delta})/n_t$. We set the transported mass to the smaller total capacity. We formed the kernel $\exp(-C_{ij}/0.02)$ and used Sinkhorn scaling with a zero-cost dummy on the lower-capacity side to absorb excess mass on the other side. We discarded the dummy couplings. The implementation checks marginal error every 20 iterations (strict tolerance $10^{-7}$, maximum 300,000 iterations, loose acceptance tolerance $10^{-4}$ in the replicate solver). For the resulting real-cell plan $\pi$, cell-specific retained fractions are $n_s\sum_j\pi_{ij}$ and $n_t\sum_i\pi_{ij}$. These fractions measure the mass actually transported. A capacity is an upper bound on that mass. ### S1.2. Sampling, expression transformation and the source graph

In each of 50 realizations we drew a Bernoulli retention indicator for each source cell and then one target index from its normalized row, $\pi_{ij}/\sum_j\pi_{ij}$. A row of zero mass has retention probability zero. The replicate analysis used matching seed 2042. We measured expression separately from the embeddings, taking log1p of nonnegative measurements and applying the section- and gene-specific cap on positive values. Zeros were preserved. On the source coordinates we connected each cell to its ten nearest other cells. Edge weights were $\exp[-d_{ij}^2/(2\sigma^2)]$, with $\sigma$ equal to the median neighbor distance (fallback 1 at zero). We averaged the directed matrix with its transpose, cleared the diagonal and normalized each row by its sum. For a matching we restricted this graph to retained cells by taking the corresponding principal submatrix. We kept its weights and recomputed the expression means on the retained cells. Thus the graph after restriction can have row sums below one.

Within-condition matching can retain tissue that is consistently lost in between-condition matching. For each section we averaged cell-specific retained fractions across its between-condition partners, using row masses when the section supplied the source and column masses when it supplied the target. The archived v4 analyses thinned within pairs from both experimental groups using the mean availability of the **source cell** of each within pair (seed 7). The November 24 implementation additionally accounts for the sampled **target partner**. Its extra retention probability is $\min(u_i,u_j)$ for source and partner availabilities $u_i,u_j$. It recomputes the induced graph and all quadratic forms after thinning. The historical files and their updated implementation share version labels. The sampling rule for a panel is therefore established from the producing run and stored tables. Note S4 reports the injury comparisons that used retention estimates for both cells in a match and identifies their panel tables. ### S1.3. Relationship to Pearson correlation, Moran's I and concordance

For each matching, write $\tilde x_m,\tilde y_m$ for centered source and matched target expression and $\bar W_m=(W_m+W_m^\top)/2$. Define $Q_x=\sum_m\tilde x_m^\top W_m\tilde x_m$ and likewise $Q_y$ and $Q_r$ for target expression and the residual $r_m=x_m-y_m$. The symmetrized cross term is $C=\sum_m\tilde x_m^\top\bar W_m\tilde y_m$. Expanding the residual gives

$$
Q_r=Q_x+Q_y-2C,\qquad
1-\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}=\frac{2C}{Q_x+Q_y}.
$$

**Pearson correlation and concordance.** For a single matched pair of vectors, ordinary Pearson $r$ is the centered cross-product divided by the geometric mean of the two sums of squares. Multiplying one vector by a positive constant leaves $r$ unchanged. For separately centered vectors, Lin's concordance instead has the arithmetic-mean scale in its denominator, $2s_{xy}/(s_x^2+s_y^2)$. Replacing these three ordinary products by the spatial products above gives $1-\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}$. In the formal special case $W=I$ and one matching, this reduces exactly to centered Lin concordance. The actual graph has zero diagonal and compares a cell with its neighbors. Centering removes the location-shift term present in the original uncentered concordance coefficient. [Lin, 1989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2720055/)

**Moran's I.** On a graph with $n$ cells and total edge weight $S_0$, $I(v)=(n/S_0)\tilde v^\top W\tilde v/(\tilde v^\top\tilde v)$. Applied to $r$, it asks whether a residual is spatially autocorrelated relative to its *own* variance. Raw NRMI scales residual structure by the spatial structures of the original source and target. Thus $I(r)$ and NRMI can give different answers even on exactly the same cells. Lee's bivariate spatial association provides a further comparison using spatial lags. Here the quadratic form uses one neighborhood operator, while a cross-product of two spatial lags would use $W^\top W$. [Moran, 1950](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/37.1-2.17) · [Lee, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/S101090100064)

For a single matching, the pattern term in S1.4 is the spatial analogue of Pearson correlation. Replacing the graph by $W=I$ gives $\rho_W=r_{\rm Pearson}=\tilde x^\top\tilde y/(\|\tilde x\|\|\tilde y\|)$. The additional amplitude factor in NRMI compares the marginal spatial strengths. Alternatively, on one common graph with nonconstant profiles and residual, substitution of the definition of Moran's $I$ gives

$$
\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}=
\frac{I(r)\|\tilde r\|^2}{I(x)\|\tilde x\|^2+I(y)\|\tilde y\|^2}.
$$

The factor $S_0/n$ cancels within that matching. When supports differ across draws, each draw contributes its own graph factor to the pooled quadratic forms. Exchanging the two fields on a fixed support leaves the scalar identity unchanged. Reversing the entire section-matching procedure can change the support and graph, and can therefore change the estimated score.

For a structured nonconstant field observed on the same graph, let $y=ax+b$ on the scoring scale. Then $Q_y=a^2Q_x$, $C=aQ_x$ and $\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}=(1-a)^2/(1+a^2)$. For $a=2$, Pearson $r=1$, $I(x-y)=I(x)$ and $\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}=0.2$. For $a=1$, an additive offset gives NRMI 0 and a constant residual for which Moran's $I$ is undefined. For $a=-1$, equal-amplitude reversal gives NRMI 2. A flat pattern on one side with nonzero spatial structure on the other gives NRMI 1. A cross term of zero also gives NRMI 1, illustrating why the scalar alone cannot distinguish a lost pattern from a change of shape. These statements apply after transformation, capping and matching. A multiplicative shift of raw counts does not generally give an exactly constant offset after log1p.

### S1.4. Pattern and amplitude decomposition

For $Q_x,Q_y>0$, define the spatial pattern agreement $\rho_W$, the amplitude agreement $\kappa$, and the signed log amplitude ratio $h$ by

$$
\rho_W=\frac{C}{\sqrt{Q_xQ_y}},\qquad
\kappa=\frac{2\sqrt{Q_xQ_y}}{Q_x+Q_y},\qquad
h=\tfrac12\log(Q_y/Q_x),\qquad
1-\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}=\rho_W\kappa.
$$

The amplitude term lies in $(0,1]$ and equals $1/\cosh(h)$. It is identical for a doubling and a halving of amplitude. The sign of $h$ recovers the direction, with $h>0$ indicating more spatial structure on the target side. At equal amplitude, $\kappa=1$ and all loss of concordance is associated with pattern. When $\rho_W>0$ and both factors are at most one, their product gives an additive loss on the logarithmic scale. We used $L_a=-\log\kappa$, $L_p=-\log[\min(1,\rho_W)]$ and the amplitude share $s=L_a/(L_a+L_p)$ when the total loss is positive.

The symmetric part of a zero-diagonal neighborhood matrix need not be positive semidefinite. Thus the Cauchy–Schwarz bound on an ordinary correlation does not apply to $\rho_W$, even if both marginal forms are positive. The identity holds for the **raw ratio**. Clipping can change its value. The replicate implementation returns a missing ratio for zero aggregate denominator. For classification, two native constant profiles give *undefined*, one constant profile gives *on/off*, and a nonpositive marginal form otherwise gives *undefined*. For positive marginal forms, $\rho_W\leq0$ is classified as pattern and $\rho_W>1$ as amplitude. In the remaining cases $s<1/3$ gives pattern, $s>2/3$ amplitude, and the intervening range mixed. At exact agreement both losses vanish, so the share is mathematically undefined; the current pair-level rule falls through to the mixed label. These cutoffs are descriptive conventions. ### S1.5. Gene eligibility and uncertainty

For the replicate statistic we applied the study-wide Moran filter to each gene on the native section graphs, then evaluated denominator strength on each sampled support. With $z$ denoting a centered expression vector here, the variance of its fixed quadratic form under independent random signs is $V(z)=\sum_{i<j}(w_{ij}+w_{ji})^2z_i^2z_j^2$. We standardized the denominator of each matching as $(q_m(x_m)+q_m(y_m))/\sqrt{V(\tilde x_m)+V(\tilde y_m)}$. A nonblank gene was evaluable in a pair if neither native profile was constant and the minimum standardized denominator over matchings was at least 1. Retention required evaluability in at least 60% of pairs after the native Moran filter. We fitted retained genes using their available scores from *all* pairs, including finite scores from pairs below the evaluability threshold. The covariate for empirical Bayes variance moderation was the mean across pairs of the log of the within-pair median standardized denominator, omitting nonfinite log values. To estimate Monte Carlo precision we resampled the 50 matchings as units 200 times (seed 0), formed and clipped a new ratio of sums for each draw, and took its sample standard deviation. This quantity conditions on the estimated plan and measured tissue.

As a numerical check, we reconstructed 2,295 finite HPOA gene–pair scores from the per-matching quadratic forms for all 15 section pairs. The maximum absolute difference from the stored raw score was below $2\times10^{-15}$. Five raw scores lay outside the clipping interval. Among the 2,293 gene–pair entries with positive marginal forms, the largest absolute error in $1-\mathrm{NRMI}_{\rm raw}=\rho_W\kappa$ was below $3\times10^{-16}$. ### S1.6. Neural-network likelihood, prior and cycle computation

For each pair we registered the concatenated normalized expression matrix with section as the SysVI batch key. The two 256-unit hidden layers injected the one-hot section covariate at each layer. The encoder's two linear heads produced a location and an unconstrained variance parameter per cell and latent coordinate. The variance activation was softplus. The standard deviation for sampling was its square root plus $10^{-12}$. The decoder had one expression-mean output per input gene and one trainable noise parameter per gene shared across cells. Only the 15 posterior means per cell were passed to the matching algorithm. For example, the naïve-5 to aggressive-34 HPOA embedding contains the 5,839 source and 4,908 target cells in that order, giving a matrix of 10,747 rows and 15 columns. [Pair-specific training](../5-Case-Study-Colitis/13-embedding-trials.py) · [Encoder and decoder, release 1.4.2](https://github.com/scverse/scvi-tools/blob/1.4.2/src/scvi/external/sysvi/_base_components.py)

The following notation specifies the scale conventions in scvi-tools 1.4.2. Write $m_{ig}$ for the decoder mean conditioned on $z_i,b_i$. In the call to PyTorch `GaussianNLLLoss`, the third argument is the decoder distribution's `scale` field. Let $v_g$ be the value of that field and $v_g^*=\max(v_g,10^{-6})$ be the value used to evaluate the likelihood. With the additive Gaussian constant omitted, the reconstruction cost for cell $i$ is

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rec},i}=
\frac12\sum_{g=1}^{G}\left[\log v_g^*
+\frac{(u_{ig}-m_{ig})^2}{v_g^*}\right].
$$

The VampPrior draws five initial pseudo-inputs from the pair's cell rows and initializes their categorical section weights from the corresponding section labels. Each pseudo-input is encoded using the same encoder in evaluation mode. Let $a_{kd}$ and $t_{kd}$ be its returned Gaussian location and scale for component $k$, coordinate $d$. The prior implementation constructs the coordinate density $f_{kd}=\mathcal N(a_{kd},t_{kd})$, where the second argument here denotes the **variance** (the release passes $\sqrt{t_{kd}}$ as the distribution's standard deviation). The weights $\omega_k$ are the softmax of five trainable logits initialized to zero. For a draw $z_i$ from the cell posterior, the implementation evaluates

$$
\widehat{\mathcal L}_{\mathrm{KL},i}
=\sum_{d=1}^{15}\left[\log q_{\phi,d}(z_{id}\mid u_i,b_i)
-\log\left(\sum_{k=1}^{5}\omega_k f_{kd}(z_{id})\right)\right].
$$

The mixture sum is evaluated within each coordinate before summation over latent dimensions. For the cycle, the module computes decoder outputs under the original and alternate section labels. The tensor actually passed to the second encoder is the original-section decoder mean, `px.loc`, paired with the alternate section index. It compares the two posterior means after joint, dimension-wise standardization across the minibatch and sums squared differences over the 15 coordinates. Thus the three loss terms sum over genes or coordinates within a cell, and the training loss averages the resulting scalar over cells. With a fixed KL weight and cycle weight 5, training used Adam ($\epsilon=10^{-2}$), shuffled minibatches and a fixed epoch limit without early stopping. The validation split held out cells from the same section pair. [Versioned model and loss](https://github.com/scverse/scvi-tools/blob/1.4.2/src/scvi/external/sysvi/_module.py) · [Versioned prior](https://github.com/scverse/scvi-tools/blob/1.4.2/src/scvi/external/sysvi/_priors.py) · [Training interface](https://github.com/scverse/scvi-tools/blob/1.4.2/src/scvi/external/sysvi/_model.py)

## S2. Controlled detection benchmarks (Figure 2)

### S2.1. Tissue geometry, expression model and truth labels (2a–2c)

The five rectilinear scenarios used a $60\times60$ lattice with four vertical domains (A–D), each 15 columns wide. For the ring scenario we retained sites within radius 34 of the center of a $68\times68$ lattice and divided the disk at radii 8.5, 17 and 25.5. Domains A–D contained equal-probability mixtures of cell types (0,1), (1,2), (2,3) and (3,0), respectively. Types were drawn separately for each section. Figure 2a illustrates the geometry and perturbations schematically. Quantitative panels used the generated count matrices.

Each experiment contained 120 genes. Eighty unchanged genes marked domains, 20 unchanged genes marked cell types, ten genes received a uniform condition effect (decoys), and ten focal genes received the spatial perturbation. All four classes had baseline spatial expression. Let $S_{ig}$ be the appropriate domain or cell-type indicator centered across the section. We drew counts from a negative binomial distribution with mean $\mu_{ig}$ and variance $\mu_{ig}+\mu_{ig}^2/\theta_g$, setting $\log\mu_{ig}=\beta_g+\alpha_gS_{ig}+\epsilon_{ig}+d_{ig}$. Here $\beta_g\sim N(1.5,0.25^2)$, $\theta_g$ is the absolute value of a $N(3,0.5^2)$ draw, and $\epsilon_{ig}\sim N(0,0.3^2)$. Focal and decoy genes used the same sequence of ten amplitudes from 1.0 to 2.4 as the domain-marked background bins. Type-marker amplitudes ranged from 1.2 to 2.2. Gene parameters were shared across strengths, conditions and replicates of a scenario. The base seed was 20261124. Section-specific type assignments, Gaussian noise and count sampling seeds were reused across strengths to support paired comparisons along the dose series.

For strengths $s\in\{0,0.5,1,1.5,2\}$, the perturbation $d_{ig}$ for decoys was $+s$ everywhere in condition 2. For focal genes we used the following six rules. Coordinates below are zero-based. At $s=0$ the designated focal identities still define the ranking diagnostic, while the true condition effect is zero for every gene.

| Spatial change | Perturbation of focal log mean |
|---|---|
| Domain DE | Add $s$ in domain A of condition 2. |
| Cell-type DE within a domain | Add $s$ only to type 0 in domain A of condition 2. |
| Within-domain shift | Add $s$ in columns 0–6 of A in condition 1 and columns 8–14 of A in condition 2. |
| Boundary band | Add $s$ in columns 14 and 15 straddling the A–B boundary in condition 2. |
| Amplitude change | Add $sS_{ig}$ in condition 2, increasing the strength of the existing domain pattern. |
| Ring domain | Add $s$ in the second concentric domain in condition 2. |

### S2.3. Donor-consistent cortical implants (2d–2e)

We kept the coordinates, manual layers and common 3,000-gene panel of the six LIBD sections. From genes passing the unperturbed LIBD Moran gate with mean count at least 0.2, we selected ten genes per spatial change and 20 decoys. Selection used the average over the three future target sections of the ratio of the largest layer mean to the section mean. Genes with ratio $\geq2$ supplied amplitude and relocation implants. Genes with ratio $<1.5$ supplied domain DE, within-layer increase and balanced redistribution. Decoys comprised ten genes from each stratum. The truth identities were held fixed across levels. Performance therefore describes recovery in a background enriched for measurable spatial structure.

For levels 1–3 the parameters $(s,a,t)$ took the values $(0.5,0.25,1/3),(1,0.5,2/3),(1.5,0.75,1)$, respectively. Within each target section let $m_l$ be its gene-specific mean in layer $l$, and $\bar m$ the mean across all spots. We set the following target means. Groups outside the specified region retained their counts.

| Implant | Target mean in the treated section |
|---|---|
| Domain DE | Multiply the mean of layer 3 by $e^s$. |
| Within-layer increase | Multiply the mean in half A of layer 3 by $e^s$. |
| Amplitude attenuation | Replace each layer mean by $\bar m+(1-a)(m_l-\bar m)$. |
| Relocation | In the ordered common layers (3, 4, 5, 6, white matter), destination index $i$ receives $(1-t)m_i+t\,m_{(i+2)\bmod 5}$. |
| Balanced within-layer redistribution | Set $m'_B=(1-t)m_B$ and $m'_A=m_A+t\,m_B n_B/n_A$ in the two halves of layer 3. This preserves the expected layer total. |
| Global DE decoy | Multiply the mean of every represented layer by $e^s$. |

For a group with original mean $m$ and target $m'$, we added independent Poisson counts of mean $m'-m$ when increasing expression. For decreases we drew binomial counts with the original count as the number of trials and probability $m'/m$. Counts and coordinates in controls were untouched. ### S3.2. Ranking overlap and whole-section label reassignment (3b–d)

For each dataset and method, we oriented scores so that a larger value indicated greater change. ### S5.1. Independent controls for amplitude and pattern (5a)

For the amplitude experiment we used the archived three-by-three spatially variable gene benchmark. The same spatial template had increasing source–target contrast gaps of 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2. At each gap, we computed the pair decomposition for each of ten focal genes and took separate medians of $\rho_W$ and $\kappa$ across the nine between-condition pairs. For the pattern experiment we fixed the correspondence to the identity and randomized each gene in the eigenbasis of a centered spatial weight matrix. Let $H=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/n$ and $H(W+W^\top)H/2=U\Lambda U^\top$ for the source 10-neighbor graph. For a centered source profile $x$ with coefficients $a=U^\top x$, we constructed $y=U\operatorname{diag}(s_j)a$ with independent signs $\Pr(s_j=-1)=p$. Both profiles have the same variance and $Q_x=Q_y=\sum_j\lambda_j a_j^2$. For $Q_x>0$ the amplitude factor is one, and

$$
\rho_W=\frac{\sum_j s_j\lambda_j a_j^2}{\sum_j\lambda_j a_j^2},
\qquad \mathbb E[\rho_W\mid x]=1-2p.
$$

On each of three source replicates we used ten focal genes, 100 random draws, and flip probabilities $p\in\{0,.05,.1,.2,.3,.4,.5,.6,.7,.8,.9,1\}$. The random seed was 20261120 plus replicate index. We generated independent uniforms for each mode, gene and draw, and reused them across probabilities to nest the flipped sets. Zero flips reproduces the source and flipping every mode reverses it. The full eigenspectrum permits intermediate individual values outside the ordinary correlation bounds. We compared observed means with the theoretical line, showed the interquartile range with 5th and 95th percentile whiskers, and displayed centered expression for Gene 60 using the first replicate and random draw on a shared scale. This experiment isolates the decomposition under known correspondence.
